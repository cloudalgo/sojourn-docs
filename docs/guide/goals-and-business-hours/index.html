    Set goals and business hours

    Give each value a goal in business minutes, and give each queue its own business hours.

    Salesforce admin About 10 minutes Since release 1

    A goal says how long a record should stay in a value. Business hours say which minutes count. Both are set in Sojourn Settings, under Time, beside Alerts, which decides who hears about a Breach.

    Set goals#

    Open Goals. Choose the Tracked object and the Tracked field; the field starts on the object's status field. Each value of the field has a row, with Any value first:

    • Target, business minutes: how long an Interval in this value should last. Leave it blank for no goal.
    • Warn at, percent: the share of the goal, from 1 to 100, at which an Interval is flagged as a warning. Leave it blank for no warning. A Goal saved any other way, from a data load or the API, is refused outside 1 to 100 too.

    A value's own goal wins over Any value. Choose Save goals. New Intervals carry the goal; Intervals already open keep the one they started with. An Interval that runs past its goal is a Breach: the Live values job flags one that is still open, and a stay is flagged when it closes past its goal.

    A warning is flagged the same way, earlier. When an Interval's business minutes reach the warn percent of its goal, Sojourn ticks Warning on the Interval and sets Warned At to the moment it got there: the time it entered the value plus that share of the goal, in its business hours. The Live values job flags an open Interval; a stay is flagged when it closes. A breached Interval is always a warning too. Once set, a flag keeps its time. A new Interval carries its warn threshold as Warn Minutes, beside Goal Minutes: the warn percent of the goal when the Interval started. Changing the goal or turning it off later changes neither the warning nor the Breach of an Interval already started. Reports on Intervals can filter on Warning, and the timeline names a warning in a stay's goal, as it names a breach.

    The Breach alerts card has its own screen, Alerts, under Time. It is for every Tracked object: its switch, Also email when a stay closes past its goal, decides whether the breach alert email also goes for stays that closed late, or only while an Interval is still open (the default). See Email a breach alert.

    Clearing a target turns the goal off. Goals are never deleted, so Intervals that point at one keep it.

    Give queues their business hours#

    Open Business hours. The card at the top names the org default, the hours used when nothing more specific applies; change it on the Overview. The Queues table lists every queue with the objects it takes. Choose business hours for a queue, or leave it on Use the next rule, then choose Save.

    For each Interval, Sojourn uses the first of these that is set. The card Which business hours an Interval uses, below the Queues table, lists the same order:

    1. The Tracked field's own business hours.
    2. A business hours field on the record, when the Tracked object names one.
    3. The queue's business hours, while a queue owns the record.
    4. The Tracked object's business hours.
    5. The Sojourn default on the Overview, then the org's default business hours.

    Business hours Salesforce cannot use (deleted or inactive) fall back to calendar time, and Sojourn logs that once.

    Changes apply to Intervals that start after you save.

    You are done when

    • An Interval created after you save shows the goal on its record.
    • An Interval for a record owned by a mapped queue counts business minutes in that queue's hours.
